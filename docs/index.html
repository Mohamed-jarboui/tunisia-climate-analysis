Comparing hourly weather-station data from Bizerte, Sousse and Kef, December 2025

Author

Mohamed Jarboui

In short

Three automatic weather stations from the Tunisia Open Data Portal, one inland and two on the coast, recorded hourly temperature, humidity, wind and solar radiation in December 2025. Over the 1,113 station-hours, and comparing only the period all three stations share (07 December 2025 to 19 December 2025):

  • Kef, inland in the northwestern highlands, is the coldest station: 10.5 °C on average against 15.4 °C in Sousse and 14.7 °C in Bizerte. It is the only one that dropped below 5 °C (40 hours, minimum 0.5 °C).
  • Kef also has the largest day-night swing: 12.1 °C between daily minimum and maximum on average, against 8.9 °C in Sousse. The sea keeps coastal temperatures steadier.
  • Bizerte is the most humid: 90.3 % relative humidity on average, above 80 % in 78.4 % of its hours.
  • Humidity moves opposite to temperature at every station (correlation from -0.46 to -0.86): it peaks around sunrise (06:00 to 08:00) and is lowest around 15:00, when temperatures peak.
  • The Kef solar sensor is faulty. It reports about 950 W/m² day and night, so its solar readings were excluded instead of making Kef look like the sunniest city.

Bizerte logs roughly every two hours, so it has fewer records over the same period. Sousse kept recording until early January; its extra weeks are used for its own time series but not in the city comparison.

Limitations

  • About two to four weeks of winter data. The results describe December 2025, not the climate of these cities. Seasonal patterns or long-term trends cannot be studied with this sample.
  • Three stations, one per city. Each station reflects its exact location (exposure, altitude, distance to the sea), not the whole city.
  • Humidity readings cap at 100 %: 73 % of Bizerte’s night-time readings are exactly 100 %, so its humidity spread is compressed.
